What can attach to this device

The Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-LCD-4, revision V4, has no free ESP32 GPIO: the RGB panel, the buses and the octal PSRAM take every pin. New hardware arrives through the 10 way H4 terminal (VIN, RS485, CAN and a level shifted I2C header), the TF slot, USB-C, the battery connectors or the 2.4 GHz radio. Most sensors plug into the I2C header; UART parts, audio, IR and sub GHz radios live on a satellite or the LAN host, which keeps to the project rule that the board renders and reports and the host decides. Difficulty means: plug in (wire it and use a stock library), driver (firmware or build work on the hub), satellite (it runs on another board or the host and the hub shows the result), not on this board (a stock V4 cannot carry it; the note says what would change that). Board facts were checked against the V4.0 schematic and Waveshare's V4 code, and several wiki claims turned out to describe V1 to V3. Before wiring anything or inserting a card, read the first six caveats.

Where things plug in

Every way into the board

Checked against the V4.0 schematic and Waveshare's V4 code. Where the wiki disagreed, the schematic won, and the confidence line says what was measured and what was derived.

ESP32-S3 GPIO

Pins
None free. GPIO 0 to 21 and 38 to 48 are all wired, 22 to 25 do not exist, and 26 to 37 serve the flash and the octal PSRAM.
Already on it
Panel on 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 to 14, 17, 18, 21, 38 to 42 and 45 to 48. I2C on 7 and 15. TF on 1, 2 and 4. CAN on 0, which is also BOOT, and 6. PWRKEY sense on 16. USB on 19 and 20. RS485 on 43 and 44.
Spare
Nothing. Every addition goes through a bus, the TF slot, USB or the radio.
Confidence
high: schematic IO table and module datasheet

H4 pins 7 to 10: I2C header

Pins
SDA GPIO15 and SCL GPIO7 through an NDC7002N level shifter. Pin 7 is I2C_VCC, an unfused tap of the 3V3 or 5V rail chosen by H3. 4.7K pull ups on both sides, 400 kHz.
Already on it
CH32V003 expander at 0x24 (backlight, LCD and touch resets, SYS_EN power latch, battery ADC), PCF85063 RTC at 0x51, and GT911 touch at 0x5D or 0x14 on the Touch SKU only. The empty U10 footprint would add a QMI8658 at 0x6B if it were ever fitted.
Spare
Every other address, including 0x3C and 0x20, which are empty on V4. Keep add on expanders off 0x20 while the port's TCA9554 fallback can write there, keep H3 at 3V3, and note that SDA and SCL have no ESD protection.
Confidence
high for wiring; H3 form and factory setting unverified

H4 pins 3 and 4: RS485

Pins
UART TX GPIO44 and RX GPIO43 into an SP3485 at 3.3 V with automatic direction switching from the TX line. The schematic IO table labels GPIO43 as RS485_TX, but the nets and both demo trees show GPIO43 receives. 120R on SW1 switch 2, 10K fail safe bias, series fuses of unspecified type, TVS diodes and a GDT to earth; not isolated.
Already on it
These are the UART0 pins, so the ROM banner and any early console output on GPIO43 fight the SP3485 output. Test pads P4 and P5 sit on the same two nets.
Spare
One half duplex pair for Modbus RTU devices at one baud rate and parity. No DE pin needs driving.
Confidence
high for pins; turnaround timing unmeasured

H4 pins 5 and 6: CAN

Pins
TWAI TX GPIO6 and RX GPIO0 through a TJA1051 that is always in normal mode. 120R on SW1 switch 1, SM24CANB TVS, ground not isolated.
Already on it
GPIO0 is also the BOOT button and a boot strap. Dominant bits read as BOOT presses, BOOT presses corrupt reception, and a bus that is dominant at reset boots the ROM downloader.
Spare
Classic CAN 2.0 up to 1 Mbps, no CAN FD. Listen only has to be set in the TWAI controller. A quiet lab bus is fine; a busy one such as a car needs a gate on the RX net during reset.
Confidence
high

GPIO16: PWRKEY sense

Pins
GPIO16, active low, with a 10K pull up to 3V3. PWRKEY also starts the discrete power latch through its own diode.
Already on it
Nothing in the port reads it yet; BOOT carries the button role.
Spare
A readable button with no power effect once SYS_EN holds the latch. Moving short and long presses here frees BOOT from its clash with CAN RX, and a very long hold can still clear SYS_EN to power off.
Confidence
high from the schematic; confirm by holding PWRKEY on USB power and reading GPIO16 low

H4 pins 1 and 2: DC input

Pins
VIN 7 to 36 V per the silkscreen (5 to 36 V in the schematic text) through a reverse polarity diode into an XL1509 buck at about 5.23 V, then an ideal diode onto USB0_5V.
Already on it
USB0_5V is also the USB-C VBUS pin and the charger input.
Spare
12 or 24 V supplies, PoE splitters and a supply shared with Modbus relays. It is the only way to put 5 V on VBUS for USB host mode.
Confidence
medium: minimum VIN unmeasured

J1 battery and BAT1 RTC cell

Pins
J1 is PH2.0 with pin 1 GND and pin 2 B+. BAT1 sits straight on the PCF85063 supply.
Already on it
An ETA6098 charger at up to 2 A (R13 82K) with STAT on EXIO0, a boost for the 5V rail on battery, and battery voltage through the CH32 ADC.
Spare
One single cell lithium pack and one rechargeable RTC cell. There is no fuel gauge.
Confidence
high for wiring; charge current from the datasheet table

TF slot

Pins
1 bit SDMMC with CLK GPIO2, CMD GPIO1 and D0 GPIO4. DAT3 on CH32 EXIO4, DAT1 and DAT2 unconnected, card detect tied to GND.
Already on it
GPIO1 and GPIO2 double as the ST7701 init lines, so the card mounts after panel init with GPIO42 held high.
Spare
One microSD card in 1 bit mode. Four bit mode is impossible.
Confidence
high

USB-C

Pins
Native USB on GPIO19 and GPIO20. Sink only with 5.1K on CC1 and CC2, and no USB to UART bridge despite the symbol name.
Already on it
The USB Serial JTAG console and flashing.
Spare
One PHY, so TinyUSB device classes or host mode replace the console. HID and MSC are in the core; NCM and host class drivers are not. VBUS reaches a device only when the board runs from VIN.
Confidence
medium: host and NCM paths untested

2.4 GHz radio

Pins
On chip Wi-Fi and BLE through the WROOM-1 PCB antenna. V4 has no u.FL option.
Already on it
The bonded NimBLE peripheral link to the Windows tray daemon.
Spare
Wi-Fi STA (unused so far), the BLE observer role (compiled out of the lcd_4 build) and ESP-NOW. Wi-Fi and BLE share the radio by time division.
Confidence
high

Pads and footprints

Pins
P4 on GPIO43 and P5 on GPIO44 (UART0 pads), TP1 to TP4 for CH32 SWD (DIO, RST, GND, 3V3), and the U10 QMI8658 footprint on the bottom side.
Already on it
P4 and P5 share the SP3485 nets, and the CH32 runs Waveshare's register firmware.
Spare
A logic analyser tap on RS485, CH32 reflashing with a WCH-LinkE, and a poll only IMU at 0x6B after LGA reflow. None is a practical expansion port.
Confidence
high

Scanning from the hub

Bluetooth and Wi-Fi scanning, on this chip

BLE scanning

Yes, with limits. The S3 controller runs the observer and peripheral roles at once, so the hub can scan while the tray daemon stays bonded, but Wi-Fi and BLE share one radio by time division and every scan window takes airtime from that link. Two things stand in the way today. The lcd_4 build compiles the observer and central roles out, and turning the observer on costs about 26 kB of flash. NimBLE-Arduino issue 1187, where a scan that kept results broke reconnection of a bonded peer, was filed on a classic ESP32 with core 2.0.17, has no maintainer fix and is unconfirmed on the S3, so run it as a regression test here: scan in short passive windows with setMaxResults(0), forward each result as it arrives, then reconnect the tray daemon and confirm the bond survives. The older crash with about 400 stored results was heap exhaustion on a starved classic ESP32 and is far less likely here, though discarding results stays good practice.

Wi-Fi access point scanning

Yes. WiFi.scanNetworks in async mode returns SSID, BSSID, channel, RSSI and auth mode for each access point. Espressif's ESP32-S3 coexistence table rates a station scan beside BLE scanning, advertising or a live BLE connection as supported and stable, and the old NimBLE report of scans failing next to BLE was closed after a clean test on core 3.3.7. Scan in short bursts, and call WiFi.persistent(false) with WIFI_STORAGE_RAM so no scan or connect writes NVS, because a flash write stalls the PSRAM scanout. The hub sends the list to the host, which aggregates it.

Passive sniffing and Kismet

A passive monitor mode is possible if it keeps management frames only. A single channel sniff can run while the station stays associated on the AP's channel; channel hopping cannot, because leaving that channel drops the host link and esp_wifi_set_channel must not be called while the station scans or connects. Sniffing while reporting over the bonded BLE link instead is a combination Espressif rates as supported but unstable, so measure it first. Continuous hopping capture stays on the host with Kismet's own hardware. Kismet has no ESP32 capture source, so the working path is scan reports: the hub sends observed access points and BLE devices as JSON to the LAN host, and a host script posts them to Kismet's Wi-Fi scan report endpoint or its BLE equivalent, the way LyndLabs Kismet-ESP32 does from a serial feed. Raw pcapng capture would need the host to synthesize radiotap headers and is unproven.

ESP-NOW for satellites

It works for satellites. While the hub holds a Wi-Fi association, ESP-NOW runs on the AP's channel and every peer must use that channel. It shares airtime with Wi-Fi and BLE, and Espressif rates ESP-NOW receive as stable only in station mode. Keep frames small, encrypt with keys the host provisions, and have the hub report what the satellites send.

The picture tears if the radio stalls PSRAM

Radio traffic does not tear the panel directly. Tearing comes from flash writes, which disable the PSRAM cache the RGB scanout reads through, and from PSRAM bandwidth contention; Wi-Fi connects, NVS saves, BLE bond writes and OTA are the usual triggers. The installed pioarduino 55.03.38 libraries ship XIP from PSRAM off and allow Wi-Fi and lwIP buffers in PSRAM (CONFIG_SPIRAM_TRY_ALLOCATE_WIFI_LWIP=y), so the documented fix is a build change: a pioarduino hybrid build whose custom_sdkconfig sets CONFIG_SPIRAM_XIP_FROM_PSRAM=y and CONFIG_SPIRAM_TRY_ALLOCATE_WIFI_LWIP=n, with the 64 byte data cache line Waveshare's own build notes list. Bounce buffers work today through bounce_buffer_size_px, the last Arduino_ESP32RGBPanel constructor argument; the port's working tree already passes a 10 line buffer, and whether it cures the observed drift has not been checked yet. CONFIG_LCD_RGB_RESTART_IN_VSYNC is already on; it recovers after a drift and does not prevent one. Until the hybrid build lands, dim the backlight around OTA and credential saves, and measure tearing on the bench in the dominant state, an idle page with periodic updates.

Left out on purpose, and it stays out:

  • Deauthentication and disassociation attacks and any Wi-Fi jamming. Section 333 of the Communications Act forbids them (FCC advisory DA 15-113), and esp_wifi_80211_tx refuses those frame types anyway.
  • Beacon floods, probe floods and fake or evil twin access points.
  • BLE advertising spam.
  • Capturing or keeping Wi-Fi data frame payloads, handshakes included. Joffe v. Google held that intercepting unencrypted Wi-Fi payloads falls under the Wiretap Act, so any sniffer keeps management frames only.
  • The offensive halves of Marauder and Bruce. Only their passive scan code is worth porting; the hub reports and never attacks.
  • Continuous channel hopping capture on the hub. It breaks the host link and loads the radio against the scanout, so capture hardware stays on the host.

Compiling other projects in

One device, many tools: how that actually works

Why whole projects do not merge

Yes, merging whole projects is hard, and mostly not worth doing. Marauder, Bruce, ESPHome and Tasmota each build one image that owns the display driver, the UI framework, the pin map, the partition table, the radio and the single main loop. Two of them cannot both own the RGB panel, the radio or loop(), and their pin maps assume boards with free GPIOs. Porting one means rewriting its display and input layers for this 480x480 RGB panel, which is most of the work, and teaching its blocking code to yield so the PSRAM scanout does not tear. The launcher approach of separate binaries with one running at a time avoids the merge but gives up the always on hub.

What works: modules

Move features, not firmwares. The multi tool projects that work share one shape: a small module interface, a build switch per module and a reporting hook. Tasmota routes every driver through one dispatch function with FUNC_INIT, FUNC_EVERY_SECOND and FUNC_JSON_APPEND and compiles drivers in with USE_ defines. ESPHome components implement setup, loop and update, and its code generator compiles only what the config selects. Meshtastic modules subclass MeshModule and register themselves, and Bruce pairs a menu class with an implementation folder per feature. Here each module is one LVGL page plus init, tick and a JSON sink. It declares what it claims (the radio, I2C addresses, RS485, TWAI, the SD card, PSRAM heavy work and a tick budget), a registry refuses clashes at boot, and a menu page lists what was built in. Modules measure and report, and the host decides. Marauder's passive Wi-Fi and BLE scans port cleanly because each is a few hundred lines over esp_wifi and the BLE scan API; its attack code stays out.

The whole API

struct ModuleCaps {
  uint8_t i2c_addrs[4];
  bool radio;
  bool rs485;
  bool twai;
  bool sd;
  bool psram_heavy;
  uint32_t tick_budget_us;
};

struct Module {
  const char* name;
  ModuleCaps caps;
  bool (*init)();
  void (*tick)(uint32_t now_ms);
  lv_obj_t* (*build_page)(lv_obj_t* parent);
  void (*to_json)(JsonObject out);
};

const Module* const kModules[] = {
#if MOD_WIFI_SCAN
  &mod_wifi_scan,
#endif
#if MOD_ENV_I2C
  &mod_env_i2c,
#endif
};

How a build picks its modules

Pick modules at build time. Each module is its own .cpp that compiles to nothing unless its flag is set, such as MOD_WIFI_SCAN=1 in the build_flags of a platformio.ini env, and the registry array is built from the same flags. One env per deployment keeps flash and RAM predictable and avoids a partition change: the BLE observer role alone costs about 26 kB of flash, and the hybrid build that turns on XIP from PSRAM is set per env as well. A registry check at boot refuses two modules that claim the same I2C address, or the radio at once. If modules later move to their own repos, lib_deps can pull them the way ESPHome's external_components does.

Read before wiring

The catches, in order of how much they can cost you

  • 01Check H3 before wiring anything to the I2C header. At 5V the level shifter's body diodes lift the ESP side of the bus to roughly 3.6 to 3.9 V (derived from the schematic, not measured), above the ESP32-S3 input limit and seen by the CH32, the RTC and touch. Measure SDA at R61 and keep H3 at 3V3.
  • 02Unplug the PC's USB cable before powering the board from VIN. The buck drives about 5.2 V onto VBUS with nothing blocking it from the connector (derived from the schematic), so it back feeds the PC port.
  • 03The charger runs at up to 2 A. Fit a single cell pack rated for that charge current, and check its PH2.0 lead order against the plus mark on the silkscreen, because pack wiring varies.
  • 04GPIO16 is the PWRKEY sense input, active low, and the power latch is SYS_EN on CH32 EXIO5; PWRKEY reaches no power IC on V4. Keep GPIO16 an input and keep bit 5 of the CH32 output register at 1. Waveshare's own init writes 0x00 to that register for 200 ms, which may drop battery power, so test it on battery with PWRKEY released before copying it.
  • 05CAN RX shares GPIO0 with BOOT and the boot strap. Move the UI button to PWRKEY before joining a live bus, start in listen only mode, and do not power the hub up on a bus that can sit dominant. The TJA1051 needs 4.5 to 5.5 V and its 5V rail is USB0_5V minus a Schottky drop, so CAN on a weak USB port is marginal.
  • 06Both V4 init paths leave EXIO0, EXIO2 and EXIO4 as outputs driven low. EXIO4 is SD DAT3, and DAT3 low at CMD0 selects SPI mode, so if the SDMMC mount fails, set EXIO4 high or make it an input first. EXIO2 is touch INT; driven low it selects GT911 address 0x5D and then fights the GT911's own INT output, so clear DIR bit 2 after reset.
  • 07SW1 terminations ship off per the wiki. Turn one on only when the hub is a physical end of that bus.
  • 08If the GT911 never answers, check the box label first: the ESP32-S3-LCD-4 variant has no GT911 fitted.
  • 09The port's expander probe falls back to a V1 to V3 TCA9554 at 0x20 when the CH32 at 0x24 does not answer, and a quick reset can make the CH32 miss its first transactions. Until that fallback sits behind a build flag or a CH32 register check, keep add on expanders off 0x20, or relays on it can switch at boot.
  • 10Anything holding SDA low locks out the CH32, and with it the backlight, the LCD and touch resets and the power latch. Keep Waveshare's bus recovery, which clocks out a stuck SDA, before CH32 init.
  • 11The I2C header has no ESD protection, unlike VIN, RS485, CAN and VBUS. Add a low capacitance ESD array at H4 before running I2C cable out of the enclosure, or carry long runs on RS485 or a differential extender.
  • 12The ROM banner and any console output before RS485 init drive GPIO43 against the SP3485 output. Claim GPIO43 as UART1 RX early in setup, or move the console to USB Serial JTAG in a hybrid build.
  • 13RS485 turnaround comes from an RC on the Q6 gate and Q6 has no part number, so the driver releases somewhere between about 56 and 186 microseconds after the last 0 bit. With 120R on, the idle bias is only about 10 to 20 mV. Bench test Modbus at the target baud before relying on it, 9600 baud especially.
  • 14On a bus that mixes classic CAN and CAN FD, the S3 treats FD frames as errors and sends error frames unless it is in listen only mode.
  • 15Minimum VIN is 7 V per the silkscreen and 5 V per the schematic text. Measure the 5V rail at 7 V and 9 V input before running near the low end.
  • 16The Waveshare wiki mixes V1 to V3 facts into its page: SW6106, TCA9554, I2C on GPIO8 and 9, TP_INT on GPIO16 and SPI mode SD. The durable port plan inherits two of them, touch INT on GPIO16 and an SW6106 at 0x3C, and a comment in the lcd_4 board.h still calls GPIO16 the power latch. On V4 touch INT is EXIO2, GPIO16 is the PWRKEY sense and the charger is an ETA6098 with no I2C.
  • 17The device token is not protected by default: the stock libraries enable neither NVS encryption nor flash encryption. HMAC based NVS encryption needs a hybrid build and an irreversible eFuse key burn, so log it in SYSTEM_CHANGE_LOG.md before burning. Token rotations are NVS writes and will disturb the panel until XIP from PSRAM is on.
  • 18Derived figures (H3 at 5V, VIN back feed, RS485 timing, JPEG decode time) come from schematic analysis and datasheets, not bench measurements. Prices are typical retail estimates; only the NeoDriver, the ReSpeaker Lite kits, the ZBT-2, the ZWA-2, the Modbus Relay (B), the Adafruit 4209 cable and the board itself were checked. Hour estimates assume an experienced embedded developer.
